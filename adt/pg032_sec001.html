Form Two

Enriched flour

Enriched flour is the one made by adding specific nutrients to it. These nutrients include B vitamins such as vitamin B1 (Thiamine), vitamin B2 (Riboflavin), vitamin

B3 (Niacin) and vitamin B9 (folic acid), and minerals like iron that are lost during the milling process.

Germ flour

Germ flour is made by mixing white flour and germ in a ratio of 3:1. Wheat germ is normally baked before being added to the flour to stop the action of enzymes and prevent fat rancidity. Cooking the germ gives the flour a malted nutty flavour. This type of flour cannot be stored for a long time due to the presence of fat which tends to oxidise easily.

Semolina

Semolina is a coarse flour made from durum wheat, a hard variety of wheat that contains more protein and gluten. It is different from the common wheat used in regular flours. Semolina has a pale yellow colour, attributed to its richness in carotenoids. Nutritionally, semolina is high in complex carbohydrates, protein, and essential minerals such as iron and magnesium.

There are various grades of semolina based on texture. Coarse semolina is primarily used to make pasta while fine semolina is commonly used in making desserts, porridges, and baked goods. Bakers also use semolina in bread, pizza crusts, and cakes to enhance texture. Semolina is a valuable plant-based source of protein and iron, making it particularly important in vegetarian and vegan diets. However, because it contains gluten, it is not suitable for individuals with gluten intolerance or celiac disease. When stored in a cool, dry place in an airtight container, semolina has a relatively long shelf life and it is less prone to spoilage compared to whole wheat flour.

Pasta is produced by mixing durum wheat semolina with water to form a stiff dough, which is then kneaded to develop gluten. The dough is shaped by extrusion or rolling and cutting, depending on the desired type of pasta. For dried pasta, the shaped dough is carefully dried under controlled conditions to reduce moisture content and ensure long shelf life. Fresh pasta skips the drying stage and is usually consumed shortly after preparation. There are different types of pasta, including macaroni, spaghetti, couscous and vermicelli, each varying in shape and culinary application.
