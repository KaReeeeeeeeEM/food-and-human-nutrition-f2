Flour

Flour is the powder obtained by milling or grinding cereals such as wheat, sorghum, maize, millet and rice. It can be categorised based on the degree of processing method and its gluten content.

Category of flour according to the degree of processing method

In this category, there are two types of flour based on the degree of processing. namely wholemeal flour and white flour.

(i) Wholemeal flour

This type of flour is made by grinding the entire grain, including the bran, germ and endosperm. It is darker in colour and more nutritious than refined flour because it contains all parts of the grain. A good example is non-dehulled maize flour.

Advantages of wholemeal flour

Wholemeal flour has the following advantages.

High in nutrients: Wholemeal flour delivers all nutrients found in whole grains.

These include B vitamins, particularly niacin and thiamine, and minerals such as zinc, iron, magnesium and manganese.

High in fibre: Being rich in dietary fibre, meals made from this flour help a person to feel full thus preventing overeating. Likewise, the fibre in whole grains supports healthy digestion by adding bulk to stool and lowering the risk of constipation. It also slows down the absorption of sugar into the blood stream, making it beneficial to people with diabetes.

Lower energy density: Wholemeal flour has a lower energy density compared to refined flour, which may help to reduce the risk of overweight and obesity.

Consequently, consuming wholemeal flour can lower the risk of non-communicable diseases (NCDs) such as heart disease and high blood pressure.

Pleasant flavour: The flour gives a pleasant nutty flavour to baked products.

Disadvantages of wholemeal flour

The following are the disadvantages of wholemeal flour.

It cannot be stored for a long time. This type of flour has a higher fat content than white flour thus increasing the risk of oxidation and rancidity which limits its shelf life.
