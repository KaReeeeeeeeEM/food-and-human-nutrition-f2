Overweight and obesity among young and school-going children is on the increase. Childhood obesity is caused by multiple factors associated with nutrition, metabolism, socioeconomic status, lifestyles, culture, psychology and genetics.

In some children, overweight and obesity

are detected during growth monitoring visits where a child’s weight is found to be above the range of reference weight of children of the same age (that is, high

weight-for-age). Some risk factors leading

to

childhood

obesity

include

overfeeding,

early

cessation

of

breastfeeding, early introduction of energy-dense family foods and over consumption of energy-dense snacks.

In addition, consuming processed sugary beverages, limited physical activities and sedentary behaviour,

such as excessive screen time may cause overweight and obesity.

Diet-Related Non-Communicable

Diseases

Diet-Related Non-Communicable

Diseases (DR-NCDs) are chronic nutrition-related

diseases

resulting

from overweight and obesity due to poor lifestyles and unhealthy eating.

Unhealthy dietary practices include increased consumption of energy dense foods (high in sugars and fats), refined or processed foods with high content of sugar, fat and salt. In addition, insufficient consumption of fruits and vegetables, legumes, whole grain or nuts and sedentary lifestyles such as physical inactivity and excessive screen time may lead to Non-Communicable

Diseases (NCDs). DR-NCDs include type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure and cardiovascular diseases such as heart attacks and strokes as well as some types of cancers like colon cancer.

These diseases are often linked to high consumption of unhealthy foods, for example, processed meats which have been smoked, salted or added with preservatives. Moreover, consumption of foods made with high amounts of saturated fats increases blood cholesterol levels. Excess consumption of common salt is associated with increased risk of hypertension. Other risk behavioural practices leading to these DR-NCDs include use of tobacco and excessive alcohol.
