(ii) Iodine Deficiency Disorder

Iodine Deficiency Disorder (IDD) is a condition resulting from inadequate intake of iodine. The body needs iodine to make thyroid hormones. These hormones control body metabolism and other important functions such as growth and development, conversion of carotene known as beta carotene

(precursor of vitamin A) into active vitamin A and in the metabolism of protein. The thyroid gland that produces thyroxine is found in the neck region. If the gland is not supplied with enough iodine, it enlarges abnormally to increase the area for iodine absorption.

This leads to a disorder known as goitre.

A person with goitre has the following signs

and

symptoms:

enlarged

thyroid, protrusion of eyeballs, easier feeling of coldness, slow thinking, poor concentration and general body weakness.

Others

include

growth

retardation, abortions and stillbirth, sleepiness, dry skin, and constipation.

Goitre can be prevented using iodine- rich foods such as sea foods, fish, dairy products, green leafy vegetables, iodine

supplements and iodised table salt. It is also important to avoid excessive intake of foods that are rich in iodine- binding agents such as cabbage, which contains goitrogens that can impair thyroid function. The treatment of goitre depends on severity. In some cases, medication or surgery is required.
