Informal table setting

This involves setting the table using few utensils just enough to serve, for example, a one-course meal. It involves minimum tableware and can be applied to a limited variety of dishes. It involves plates, glassware, cutleries and a tablecloth. Flowers can be included for decoration.

The following are important considerations when setting a table for a meal.

(i)

The table should be thoroughly

cleaned and covered with a neat tablecloth.

(ii)

Crockeries and cutleries should

be

thoroughly

cleaned

and

properly dried before use.

(iii) The tablecloth should be evenly spread over all sides of the table.

(iv) Table mats should be placed on the table to protect the tablecloth from heat and food stains.

(v)

Dinner plates should be placed at

the centre of the table mat.

(vi) Lay the napkin on the left side of the plate and place the fork on top of the napkin.

(vii) Place the knife close to and on the right side of the plate with its blade pointing in.

(viii) If soup is to be served, the spoon should be placed beside the table knife on the right-hand side. If a dessert dish is to be served, the spoon and fork can be placed horizontally above the dinner plate. The handle of the spoon should face the right side, and the fork handle the left side.

(ix) Teaspoons should be placed on the saucer, under the handle of the cup on the right side.

(x)

The side plate should be put on

the left with a well-folded napkin beside or under the fork.

(xi) A glass of drinking water or juice should be placed on the right- hand above the spoon and the table knife.

(xii) The dinner plate should be placed in-between the fork and the table knife or spoon. Other items such as serving spoons, salt and pepper shakers should be placed in convenient places.

The saltshaker should be placed on the right of the pepper shaker.

Note: If a person is left-handed, the knife and spoon are laid on the left side of the table and the fork on the right side. Figure 4.3 illustrates more about informal table setting.
