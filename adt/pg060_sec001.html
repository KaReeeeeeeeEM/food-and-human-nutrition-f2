Undernutrition

Under-nutrition is caused by inadequate energy or other nutrients to meet an individual needs for maintaining good

health. This may result from limited food

availability, poor food choices or poor

absorption of nutrients in the body. Under-

nutrition occurs in different sub-forms, namely wasting, stunting, underweight

and micronutrient deficiencies. These sub-

forms are described as follows. a) Wasting

Wasting, or low weight-for-height, is a condition where a person becomes too thin for their height due to rapid weight loss or an inability to gain weight. It results from short-term inadequate food intake or illness and is one of the key signs of acute malnutrition. Acute malnutrition is a form of under-nutrition caused by a sudden decrease in food consumption or illness. It can present as wasting and/or bilateral pitting oedema, also called nutritional oedema.

Acute malnutrition is further classified into

Moderate Acute

Malnutrition

(MAM) and Severe Acute Malnutrition

(SAM) according to the degree of wasting and the presence or absence of nutritional oedema. MAM is moderate weight loss or wasting and no nutritional oedema. SAM is a severe wasting condition that is characterised by a massive loss of body fat and muscle tissue and/or presence of nutritional oedema. Nutritional oedema is caused

by fluid retention and swelling starting from the feet or legs that can progress to other parts of the body, including the face.

(i)Acute malnutrition with oedema

Acute malnutrition with oedema is a severe form of under-nutrition where a person suffers from sudden weight loss and develops swelling (oedema), usually in the feet, legs, or face. This swelling is caused by fluid retention due to a lack of protein in the diet. It is a life- threatening condition that needs urgent treatment. When pressure is applied on the feet, it leaves a depression or pit in the tissues due to swelling of the feet. This condition is known as bilateral pitting oedema or nutritional oedema. Acute malnutrition with oedema previously known as kwashiorkor is common in children during the rapid growth period, between one to five years.

Signs and symptoms of acute malnutrition with oedema

The signs and symptoms of acute

malnutrition with oedema include bilateral

pitting oedema on legs, arms and face;

light-coloured and thinned hair; a potbelly;

a swollen moon face; and a shiny, pale and

peeling skin, which later forms wounds.

Others include tiredness, irritability, loss of

appetite and anaemia in severe cases, regular

infections, misery and apathy, frequent diarrhoea and impaired growth. Figure
