Poor maternal and childcare

Women and children have special nutritional needs that must be met to prevent malnutrition. Improving their diets, providing adequate care and providing access to nutrition services such as nutritional counselling, antenatal care and micronutrient supplementation are critical to prevent malnutrition.

During

pregnancy,

meals

lacking

essential nutrients such as iron, folate and iodine may cause anaemia, stillbirth, low birthweight and developmental delays of the foetus. Other factors including excessive workload, short pregnancy intervals, low education level, poor economic status, limited access to and control over the available resources, limited childcare facilities at the workplace, inadequate social support from spouses, families and communities as well as inappropriate cultural beliefs and habits, all impact the ability to provide adequate care to women and children. Increased workload to women hinders them from accomplishing child

and family care responsibilities; for instance, they lack sufficient time to breastfeed and prepare nutritious meals.

Excessive workload limits women’s personal care and it increases the risk of poor nutritional status and health.

This may lead to poor pregnancy outcomes such as inadequate maternal weight gain, premature birth and low birthweight (baby born weighing less than 2.5 kilograms). Low birthweight babies have low immunity, high risk of contracting infectious diseases, increased risk of developmental delays, mental retardation as well as hearing and visual impairments.

In children, adequate care is important to ensure good nutrition and health status. Childcare practices such as early initiation of breastfeeding, exclusive breastfeeding

up

to

six

months,

continued breastfeeding up to two years of life or beyond and timely introduction of complementary feeding on nutrient- rich foods are all key to proper growth and development of a child. Caregivers should consider the quantity and quality of food given to the child as well as the frequency of meals to ensure optimal growth. Sanitation, personal hygiene and attendance to RCH clinics for immunization and growth monitoring are also essential for child development.

If these are not observed, under nutrition and the risk of diseases and death among children will increase.
