Food and Human Nutrition

Form Two

Lactating mothers: Nutritional requirements increase more during lactation

than

during

pregnancy.

Therefore, lactating mothers should eat a variety of foods and maintain a well-balanced diet. This will enable a lactating mother to produce enough breast milk for the baby and fulfil her own nutritional requirements while remaining with good health. When the intake of energy and other nutrients does not increase during lactation, the woman’s own reserves are used, leaving her undernourished. Optimal feeding practices during the postnatal period reduces the risk of illness, malnutrition and death.

The elderly: Although older adults need lower energy, their nutrient needs remain the same or even increase for some of them, resulting in the need for consuming more nutrient-dense foods.

As a person ages, their body system’s ability to work tends to drop. Several challenges are associated with age.

These include loss of taste, inability to chew, and loss of appetite. These challenges may increase the risk of malnutrition. Poverty, functional disability, dependency, advanced age and dementia are some of the physical conditions that potentially place older adults at an increased nutritional risk.

Older people should be provided with easy-to-chew, diverse and nutritious foods in their diets. Malnutrition in older

people should be prevented early to reduce the risk of illness and premature death.

Sick people: Sickness increases the risk of malnutrition by reducing a person’s appetite and general food intake. Sickness can also impair nutrient metabolism in the body. Reduced appetite hinders the person from taking enough food, which is necessary for the body to function properly. This will cause inadequate dietary intake, leading to malnutrition.

Refugees: These are people who have been forced to leave their country to escape a war, prosecution or natural disaster. Under this situation, they cannot settle to produce or acquire food, a situation which increases their risk of being malnourished. These may adversely affect feeding and care, including breastfeeding of infants and young children. Refugees should be supplied with nutrient-dense and diverse food in their camps to ensure that they stay in a good health.

Effects of malnutrition in the community

Malnutrition has many negative effects in the community. These include poor school performance, decreased productivity, increased risk of NCDs, illnesses and deaths. Others include increased cost of treatment, development of disabilities and progression of malnutrition from one generation to another.
