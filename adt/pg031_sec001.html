It is less susceptible to mycotoxin contamination. Mycotoxins tend to be mostly concentrated in the bran, which is removed in the process of producing white flour.

Disadvantages of white flour

The following are the disadvantages of white flour.

Insufficient nutrients: During milling, white flour loses essential nutrients such as protein, B-vitamins and minerals such as calcium and iron.

Low dietary fibre: White flour has readily available sugar due to low fibre content.

When one consumes white flour, the sugar present is rapidly absorbed, contributing to overweight and obesity. Likewise, white flour increases the risk of type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Increase the risk of cardiovascular diseases: Consuming large amounts of white flour can lead to overweight and obesity which increases the risk of heart diseases since most of the fibre for slowing down the absorption of sugar is reduced during milling.

Lower bowel movement: Dietary fibre helps to regulate bowel movement. In contrast, white flour contains very low quantities of dietary fibre; thus, it increases the chances of constipation.

Other types of flour obtained from either whole grain or white flour include self- rising flour, enriched flour, germ flour and semolina. These are described as follows.

Self-rising flour

Self-rising flour is a type of flour that already contains a baking powder thus eliminating the need for additional raising agents in baking. It is typically made from refined white wheat flour. A common formulation involves mixing 6 g of baking powder with 125 g of wheat flour, whose ratio is approximately 1:21 to give a sufficient raising action. Self-rising flour is suitable for making plain cakes and scones.
