Batter

Batter is a mixture of flour, liquid and eggs. Rice and wheat flour can be used. The liquid may include water, milk or coconut milk. Eggs in the batter play the roles of binding, trapping air and enriching the mixture. In making batter, the mixture should be beaten well to smoothen it and incorporate air as a raising agent. Besides beating, air can be incorporated into the batter through sieving the flour. The trapped air expands when the mixture is heated. These raising agents give a light texture to the food after cooking. The methods used in cooking batter dishes include baking and frying. Baked batter dishes are light and digestible. Furthermore, the steam produced during the cooking process acts as a raising agent. Other kinds of raising agents such as baking powder and yeast may also be added, depending on the type of dishes to be prepared. For example, bhajia, rice buns and banana fritters use baking powder as a raising agent.

Types of batter

The types of batter depend on the consistency of the mixture. This consistency depends on the amount of liquid used in making the batter. The mixture should be covered and allowed to stand for at least ten minutes in a cool place for the liquid to be absorbed by the starch granules before using it. There are three types of batter, namely thin, medium or coating and thick batter, which is also known as fritter batter.

Thin batter: This type of batter is used to make food dishes like pancakes, pancake sachet, Yorkshire pudding and toad in the hole. It is also used to bind foods such as samosas as well as to thicken sauces and stews.
