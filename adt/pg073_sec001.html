Food and Human Nutrition

Form Two

Excessive dietary intake provides the body with a higher amount of nutrients than the required amount for normal growth, development and metabolism.

Excessive dietary intake, especially of high energy and sugary foods, may lead to overweight and obesity.

Diseases

Diseases may affect an individual’s appetite and ability to eat. As a result, the individual will eat less, minimise food choices or skip certain food types. Conditions such as vomiting and diarrhoea may impair nutrient absorption and utilization, rendering the body vulnerable to diseases. Infectious diseases such as malaria, pneumonia, measles,

diarrhoea,

acquired

immunodeficiency syndrome (AIDS) and worm infestations can cause under- nutrition. This is because sick people may have reduced dietary intake or impaired absorption and utilization of nutrients. A sick person has increased nutrient needs which may not be met during sickness. Young children have weak body immunity to fight against infections. Hence, lack or incomplete vaccinations increase their susceptibility to infectious diseases which may lead to malnutrition.

Poor economic status of the family

Poor economic status of the family is one of the main causes of malnutrition because it can lead to inadequate provision of necessities of life such as food. Families with low economic status

have limited access to nutritious foods including fruits, vegetables, legumes, meat, eggs, fish and fortified foods making it difficult to meet their dietary needs. In addition, when individuals are unable to afford a diversified and balanced diet, they are more likely to suffer from malnutrition. People living in poverty often face financial limitations which hinder their access to safe, sufficient and nutritious foods.

They may be aware of the right kind of foods to eat but not being able to afford.

Consequently, they consume more affordable, high-energy staple foods rather than diverse nutritious foods.

They opt for cheap food that they can afford.

Shortage of food in the family

Food shortage is a situation in which the amount of food available is insufficient to meet the dietary needs of the individuals. Availability of food in the family can be influenced by various factors. One of them can be low income to access nutritious foods and markets that offer a wide variety of fresh and healthy food options. Other factors are those affecting food production including poor climatic conditions like unreliable rainfall, long-term drought or floods, limited or lack of access to agricultural inputs such as fertilizers and good quality seeds, pest and diseases affecting crops and livestock. Families become more vulnerable to malnutrition
