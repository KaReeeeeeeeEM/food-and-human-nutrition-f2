A three-course meal: This kind of meal comprises with an appetiser, a main course meal and a dessert. The appetiser is the first course; the main course meal is the second course and dessert is the third course. An example of a three- course meal is as follows:

1st – course :

Vegetable broth

2nd – course : Coconut rice, stewed kidney, braised peas and carrots

3rd – course: Banana fool

Note: Sometimes, meals may include side

dishes, also known as accompaniments.

These are complimentary items served alongside the main course or at various

points during the meal. Examples include,

bread and butter served before the meal, small sweets served at the end, or items

like salad or chutney served with the main

dish but not listed as separate courses.

Factors to consider when planning for family meals

Meals should be planned to ensure that all family members receive appropriate foods with the right balance of nutrients to meet their dietary preferences and nutritional needs. The following should be considered when planning family meals.

Balanced meals

A balanced meal is obtained by combining dishes made from different food groups to provide the body with

nutrients

such

as

proteins,

carbohydrates, vitamins, healthy fats and minerals to support the well-being of individuals. Roughage should also be considered during meal planning as they aid digestion.

Food variety

Food variety is important to prevent monotony and nutrient deficiencies.

Including a range of foods make meals appealing and appetising. This variety can also be achieved by using different cooking methods.

Foods in season

It is wise to choose foods that are in season because they are fresh, nutritious, cheap and locally available. Off-season foods tend to be expensive. Besides, most of them may lack freshness because they have been processed or stored. Examples of seasonal foods are vegetables and fruits.
